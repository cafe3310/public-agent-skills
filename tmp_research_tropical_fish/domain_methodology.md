# Domain Methodology for Home Tropical Fish Water Quality Regulation

## 1. Standard Frameworks for Water Quality Analysis
To analyze aquarium water quality effectively, aquarists and professionals utilize a 5-phase systematic framework:

### Phase 1: Scoping & Baseline Definition
- **System Classification**: Identify the aquarium type (Freshwater Tropical, Planted, Cichlid). Each has vastly different "safe" ranges.
- **Inhabitant Profiling**: Document specific pH, hardness (GH/KH), and temperature requirements.
- **Source Water Analysis**: Test tap or RO/DI water to establish a baseline.

### Phase 2: Monitoring Strategy
- **Tier 1 (Critical/Weekly)**: Ammonia ($NH_3$/$NH_4^+$), Nitrite ($NO_2^-$), Nitrate ($NO_3^-$), pH, and Temperature.
- **Tier 2 (Stability/Monthly)**: Carbonate Hardness (KH), General Hardness (GH).
- **Tier 3 (Advanced/Diagnostic)**: Phosphates ($PO_4$), Calcium, Magnesium, TDS, and Dissolved Oxygen (DO).

### Phase 3: Analysis & Interpretation
- **Trend Analysis**: Analyze trends over weeks rather than single snapshot readings.
- **Interdependency Mapping**: e.g., Ammonia is more toxic at higher pH levels; Low KH (below 3 dKH) makes the system vulnerable to pH crashes.

### Phase 4: Intervention & Mitigation
- **Dilution**: Partial water changes (20–30%) to reduce nitrates and replenish minerals.
- **Buffering**: Use natural buffers (crushed coral to raise pH/KH, driftwood/peat to lower it) rather than chemical pH adjusters.

### Phase 5: Documentation & Review
- **Logbook Maintenance**: Record all test results, water changes, and livestock additions.

*Confidence/Precision:* High. This methodology aligns with standard professional and advanced hobbyist practices.
*Sources:* aqualabaquaria.com, aqua-fish.net

## 2. Biological Metrics: Nitrogen Cycle Efficiency
Efficiency is measured by direct chemical indicators (steady state) and operational metrics.

### Direct Chemical Metrics
- **Ammonia ($NH_3$/$NH_4^+$)**: Target is **0 ppm**. Detectable ammonia indicates a cycle crash or insufficient biofiltration.
- **Nitrite ($NO_2^-$)**: Target is **0 ppm**. Presence suggests *Nitrobacter/Nitrospira* are failing to keep up with *Nitrosomonas*.
- **Nitrate ($NO_3^-$) Accumulation Rate**: Direct metric of total bioload. Target is <20 ppm for sensitive species, <40-50 ppm for hardy fish.

### Operational Metrics (Advanced/RAS)
- **Volumetric TAN Conversion Rate (VTR)**: Grams of Total Ammonia Nitrogen (TAN) removed per cubic meter of filter media per day ($g/m^3/day$).
- **Specific Surface Area (SSA)**: Available living space for bacteria.
- **Dissolved Oxygen (DO)**: Nitrification is aerobic. Efficiency drops if DO falls below **5 ppm**.
- **Carbonate Hardness (KH)**: Nitrifying bacteria consume carbonates (~7.14 mg of alkalinity per 1 mg of ammonia oxidized). If KH is 0, the cycle stalls.

*Confidence/Precision:* Very High (Quantitative thresholds widely supported by aquaculture science).
*Sources:* aquariumcoop.com, globalseafood.org, aquariumindustries.com.au

## 3. Chemical Criteria & Toxicity (LC50 Values)
The LC50 (Lethal Concentration, 50% over 96 hours) for ammonia is measured in un-ionized ammonia ($NH_3$), which is highly toxic, as opposed to Total Ammonia Nitrogen (TAN).

### 96-h LC50 for Common Tropical Fish
| Fish Species | 96-h LC50 ($NH_3$ in mg/L) | Sensitivity Level |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cardinal Tetra** (*P. axelrodi*) | 0.36 | Very High |
| **Goldfish** (*C. auratus*) | 0.51 | High |
| **Angelfish** (*P. scalare*) | 0.58 | High |
| **Guppy** (*P. reticulata*) | 1.17 – 1.26 | Moderate |
| **Common Carp** (*C. carpio*) | 1.74 – 1.85 | Moderate |
| **Tilapia** (*O. niloticus*) | 2.35 – 3.30 | Low (Tolerant) |
| **Betta** (*B. splendens*) | ~7.7+ | Very Low (Tolerant)* |
*\*Labyrinth fish tolerate higher levels by breathing atmospheric air.*

### Toxicity Thresholds
- **Safe Level**: < 0.02 mg/L $NH_3$
- **Chronic Stress Level**: 0.05 mg/L $NH_3$ (causes gill lesions, suppressed immunity).
- **Acute Danger**: > 0.10 mg/L $NH_3$.

*Note on pH*: Toxicity shifts dramatically with pH. At higher pH (alkaline), more TAN converts to toxic $NH_3$. For every 1-point increase in pH, $NH_3$ concentration increases roughly tenfold.
*Confidence/Precision:* Very High (Scientific LC50 empirical data).
*Sources:* nano-reef.com, researchgate.net

## 4. Establishing the Nitrogen Cycle: Timelines & Inoculation
Establishing the biological filter typically takes **2 to 8 weeks** depending on the method.

### Cycle Timeline (Without Inoculation)
1. **Stage 1: Ammonia Spike (Days 1–7)**: Ammonia rises >0.5 ppm.
2. **Stage 2: Nitrite Spike (Days 7–21)**: *Nitrosomonas* bacteria convert ammonia to nitrite. Nitrite spikes, ammonia drops.
3. **Stage 3: Nitrate Rise (Days 21–42)**: *Nitrospira* convert nitrite to nitrate.
4. **Cycle Complete (Week 4–8)**: Ammonia: 0 ppm, Nitrite: 0 ppm, Nitrate: >5 ppm.

### Bacterial Inoculation Methods
- **Seeded Filter Media (Fastest)**: Transferring used media from a healthy tank. Reduces cycling time to **3–7 days**.
- **Commercial Bottled Bacteria**: High-concentration refrigerated starters (e.g., FritzZyme TurboStart). Reduces cycling to **1–2 weeks**.
- **Substrate & Decor Transfer**: Moderate effectiveness, as most beneficial bacteria reside in high-flow filter areas.

*Confidence/Precision:* High (Standard hobbyist benchmarks).
*Sources:* petco.com, apifishcare.com, co2art.us
