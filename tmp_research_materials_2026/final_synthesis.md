## 1. Consumer Electronics (Task 1)
By 2026, consumer electronics will see major shifts in battery technology, thermal management, and display form factors.

*   **Battery Density Leap**: The wearables market is adopting all-solid-state batteries (SSBs) rapidly. For instance, TDK's CeraCharge material achieves a 1,000Wh/L density (a 100x increase), intended to replace coin-cell batteries in smartwatches and hearing aids by 2026. Smartphones are leaning towards semi-solid-state solutions, allowing massive capacities (e.g., 10,200mAh) in standard phone frames (gizmochina.com).
*   **Graphene Integration**: Graphene is maturing past the hype phase. In thermal management, graphene-diamond composite materials are being deployed to lower computing temperatures by ~20°C (openpr.com). In displays, it is substituting Indium Tin Oxide (ITO), enabling flexible screens under 0.1mm thick that can withstand 500,000 bending cycles (graphenerich.com).

## 2. Smart Clothing and Textiles (Task 2)
Fabrics are transitioning from passive coverings to active, self-regulating devices, largely driven by nanotechnology and biomaterials.

*   **Self-Powering & Self-Cleaning**: Fabrics integrating 2D nanomaterials (MXene) and conductive polymers can convert body heat and solar energy into electricity, eliminating battery packs for wearables. Simultaneously, TiO2 nanoparticle treatments enable cotton to break down organic stains and kill bacteria when exposed to UV light (oasislaundry.com).
*   **Thermoregulation via SMAs**: Shape Memory Alloys (SMAs) integrated into fabrics can change their physical structure based on temperature. When exposed to heat, fibers expand to create insulating air layers, boosting thermal resistance by up to 50% (researchgate.net).
*   **Bio-integrated Textiles**: Fungal mycelium grown on gauze is creating conductive "vegan leather" that monitors heart and brain signals with medical-grade accuracy. Looking towards 2026, we are also seeing the introduction of self-healing textiles using micro-encapsulated resins that restore up to 70% of fabric strength when torn (heuritech.com).
